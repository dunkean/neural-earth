# Hydrology geometry: river vectors, shores, water fields and surface envelopes

Module: `backend/terrain_hydrology_geometry.py` (`GEOMETRY_VERSION = 'hydro-geometry-v1'`).
Tests: `tests/python/test_terrain_hydrology_geometry.py`. Design: [multiscale hydrology](../design/multiscale-hydrology.md).

The module turns authoritative core records (reaches, lakes, grids) into display/physics geometry. It refines geometry only: it never creates a receiver field, a new basin or a new confluence, and it never imports the server, Torch or CUDA.

## Conventions and units

- Map metres, x east, y south. `bounds = (x0, y0, x1, y1)` are cell-edge extents, as in `terrain_coarse.CoarseGrid`; cell `(r, c)` has its centre at `(x0 + (c + 0.5) dx, y0 + (r + 0.5) dy)`.
- Lengths, widths, depths and wavelengths of rivers are physical metres; levels are metres; flow is m³/s. With `sphere_radius_m` (for the default world, `40e6 / (2 pi)`), east-west distances are scaled by `cos(latitude)`, `latitude = -y / R`, clamped at `1e-3` near the poles. Reaches are refined in a local frame `X = (x - xc) cos(lat(y))`, `Y = y - yc` and returned in map metres.
- Inputs: reach `id, source, target, basin_id, xy (N, 2), water_m (N), flow_m3s, area_m2, downstream_id`, optional `pins` (indices of portals/nodes in `xy`), `corridor_m`, `tolerance_m` (cap on the default tessellation error), `climate`; lake `id, level_m, closed, area_m2, cells`; core grid `height`/`height_m`, `lake_ids`, `ocean`, `bounds`.
- Outputs are float64 internally. `to_local_float32` / `pack_reaches` split positions into a float64 origin (snapped to 1024 m) and float32 offsets; a round trip at |x| = 1.9e7 m measured 1.5e-5 m error.

A refined reach keeps the record keys and adds `xy`, `station_m` (channel arc length), `axis_station_m` (stable valley station), `water_m`, `width_m`, `depth_m`, `tangent`, `pins`, `belt_xy`, `belt_station_m`, `belt_water_m`, `belt_width_m` (area-conserving far width), `belt_halfwidth_m`, `wavelength_m`, `bankfull_width_m`, `bankfull_depth_m`, `corridor_m`, `morphology` (`straight`, `meandering`, `nested`) and `diagnostics` (profile conflict, valley-fit shift and climb check, sinuosity, limiter scale, nested intensity).

Water fields: `class` uint8 (0 dry, 1 ocean, 2 lake, 3 river: the kind with larger coverage), `coverage`, `water_level_m` (NaN dry), `depth_m`, `flow_x`/`flow_y` (m³/s times the downstream unit tangent), `river_coverage`, `distance_m` (to the nearest river centreline; `distance_limit_m` outside the search band), `width_m`, `bankfull_depth_m`, `river_level_m`, `resolved` (0-1 section resolvability), `reach_index`, `still_coverage`, `still_level_m`, `lake_index`, per-row physical `cell_m`.

## Methods

**Display courses.** Regional hydrologic records are cut wherever a potential tributary may join. Once annual-dry tributaries are hidden, those cuts become artificial pins every few cells. A record shorter than its wavelength (`11 W`, about 8 km for 10,000 m³/s) cannot bend, and its tapered ends keep the D8 corners. `compact_display_reaches` joins record `A` with `B = A.downstream_id` only when all of these hold: both are listed, they share `parent_id` and kind (trunk by default), and `A` is the only listed record draining into `B`, so no displayed tributary joins the interior. `A` must not end at a lake or a portal. The junction point and level must agree within 1 mm, and annual and monthly flows must agree within 1 %. `navigation_months` must be identical, as a list or an array, or absent on both. A flow, regime or navigation change keeps the cut instead of mixing records or relabelling a navigable section. The merged record keeps the head's `id`, `source`, start portal/tangent and navigation, which every member shares. It takes the tail's `target`, `downstream_id`, flow, area (the drained area at the downstream end) and end portal/tangent/kind. `length_m` is the sum over members. `slope` is the whole-course drop between the end water levels divided by that length. Both are dropped if any member lacks a length. The duplicated junction vertex is dropped, only true interior pins survive, and `members` lists the original ids. Downstream ids pointing into a chain are remapped. A plain chain walk, no scene graph.

**Axis.** The parent course (for example D8 cell centres) is simplified by Douglas-Peucker at `max(spacing / 2, W / 2)` with ends and pins retained. A channel cannot express staircase detail finer than its half-width, and such corners would otherwise cap every bend through the `0.7 / κ_axis` bound. The simplified course is then sampled as a Hermite/Bezier spline with bisector tangents and centripetal handles shorter than 0.85/3 of each chord. Controls stay exact. `start_tangent`/`end_tangent` override node directions; `refine_network` sets the bisector of the largest inflow and the outflow at each node, so a main stem is C1 through its nodes. A parent-length coordinate `q(s)` maps axis stations back to the input record.

**Valley fit (optional, with terrain).** A Viterbi path over 17 lateral offsets inside the inherited corridor (`corridor_m - W/2`) at terrain-scale stations. Node cost is height above the section minimum in bankfull depths plus `0.25 (o / corridor)²`; transitions cost `2 (Δo / Δs)²` and lateral moves steeper than 0.7 are forbidden. Pins are forced to zero offset with a C1 taper. The fit is rejected (and reported) if its mean ground is higher than the parent axis or its cumulative adverse rise exceeds the axis value by more than 10 % plus one depth. The search never leaves the corridor and does not smooth stairs without terrain.

**Axis bend radius.** A wide channel drawn on the Hermite axis (turning concentrated at its controls) and on the valley-fit lattice path (turning concentrated at its lateral moves) shows straight chords joined by kinks tighter than the channel itself. After the valley fit, the smoothed axis therefore receives at most four Gaussian passes of `σ = 0.75 W` on its physics-fixed stations. A pass runs only while the curvature outside the pin tapers exceeds `1 / (1.5 W)`, the same minimum radius as the bends. Each pass is blended in with a C1 weight that is zero, with zero slope, within `3 W` of every pin, so node positions and tangents stay exact. This only lowers curvature and adds no bends. The unsmoothed parent-polyline fallback of crossing repair is left as is. `diagnostics['axis_min_radius_m']` reports the result, including the pin tapers.

**Water profile.** Levels are made non-increasing per pinned interval by pool-adjacent-violators least squares, clipped between the pin levels. Pin levels that rise downstream are lowered to the running minimum and the rise is reported as `profile_conflict_m`. With terrain, the profile can only be lowered toward the smoothed fine floor (`cummin`), and pin levels stay exact.

**Width and depth.** `W = 7.2 Q^0.5`, `D = 0.27 Q^0.39` is the practical power-law prior also used by [BasinMaker](https://basinmaker.readthedocs.io/en/latest/basinmaker_tools.html) and [GLOBAL-FATE, equation 11](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/12/5213/2019/gmd-12-5213-2019.html). [Andreadis, Schumann and Pavelsky (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1002/wrcr.20440) supplies the global bankfull-geometry context; this implementation does not claim to reproduce its fitted exponents verbatim. The minimum width is 0.5 m and the minimum depth 0.05 m. The core supplies annual mean Q, rather than observed bankfull discharge, so these dimensions are heuristic. Width is constant per reach, so steps occur at confluences.

**Bends.** Channel wavelength `λ = 11 W` follows the meander-wavelength scaling of [Leopold and Wolman (1960)](https://doi.org/10.1130/0016-7606(1960)71[769:RM]2.0.CO;2). The channel direction angle relative to its axis is integrated along channel arc length (`da = cos θ ds'`, `do = sin θ ds'`), as in the sine-generated curve of [Langbein and Leopold (1966)](https://doi.org/10.3133/pp422H). The carrier is not a sinusoid: it is C2 cubic B-spline noise of a phase whose frequency is modulated by smooth value noise, with amplitude modulation. Hashing, noise and modulation are adapted from Magna `hydrology_meanders.rs`. Angle content longer than about 0.45 λ is removed so the integral does not random-walk. A weak restoring term keeps the offset within about 4 W, the turn rate is capped at `1 / (1.5 W)` (exact minimum bend radius 1.5 W), and `|θ| <= 1.35 rad`. The course is therefore a single-valued graph over its axis and cannot loop or self-intersect. Mobility `1 / (1 + (S / S_t)²)` uses the [Leopold and Wolman (1957)](https://doi.org/10.3133/pp282B) meandering/braiding threshold `S_t = 0.0125 Q^-0.44` (SI units), so steep reaches stay nearly straight. Offsets are softly clipped (`b tanh(o / b)`) to the free corridor, bounded by the valley wall half-width from terrain and by `0.7 / κ_axis`. The bound is smoothed by a minimum filter of ±λ/2 and a Gaussian, so one confined section does not straighten 16 km of a large river. Valley walls rise `2 D` above the bank level just outside `max(W / 2, half a terrain cell)`, or above bed + `2 D` where that bank is already high, as in a canyon. The channel's own width and a one-cell incised parent trench are therefore not walls. They are C1-tapered to zero at every pin, so shared endpoints and tangents are exact. A local limiter catches composed curvature above `1 / (1.125 W)`.

**Nested tropical belts (optional).** Eligibility multiplies smooth gates: precipitation 1.2-2 m/yr, mean temperature 18-22 °C, slope below 2e-4 to 6e-4, Q 20-200 m³/s and valley half-width 3-8 W. Intensity varies by reach hash, and values below 0.15 are dropped, so humid flat reaches differ and most rivers are untouched. A belt offset band (`λ_b = 55 W`, amplitude about `5 W` times intensity) carries the channel. A short band (`3.5 W`, 0.35 rad times intensity) adds nested bends to the channel angle. The morphology description follows compound loops ([Brice, 1974](https://doi.org/10.1130/0016-7606(1974)85%3C581:EOML%3E2.0.CO;2)). There is no migration, bar, oxbow or secondary channel model.

**LOD.** Dense stations depend only on reach physics (`λ / 24`, finer for nested reaches, bounded by length / 200000). `tolerance_m` and `simplify_reach` select vertices of the same dense curve. In `rasterize_water`, each reach blends from the channel (pixel at most λ/4) to the belt (pixel at least λ), using `smoothstep` of pixel size. The belt's effective width `W × local sinuosity` (capped by its envelope) conserves wet area, so far pixels receive partial coverage instead of a flooded belt.

**Crossings.** `refine_network` processes reaches in id order. An axis-aligned bounding-box sweep considers each segment pair once and applies the exact proper-intersection test in the same compiled (Numba) loop. It sweeps along the axis on which fewer box intervals overlap. Unlike median-sized spatial bins, this handles long main-stem segments beside tiny tributary segments without inserting the same pair into thousands of bins. When centrelines cross away from a shared node, it repairs the crossing. Intersections within the two half-widths of a common node count as joining. So do intersections near the mouth of a reach whose `downstream_id` is the other reach, because a regional tributary may join a cell after the receiver's inserted portal point. For such a crossing, it damps the lower-flow reach: half bends, no bends, no valley fit, then the unsmoothed parent polyline. If the smaller reach is exhausted, the larger reach is damped the same way. Crossings already present in the parent polylines are reported as `inherited_crossings`.

**Parent preservation.** `preserve_parent` requires complete parent blocks on the global lattice: all project lattices share origin 0, so fine index `i` has parent `i // factor`, with factors 32 from 7680 m to 240 m and 8 from 240 m to 30 m. `aligned_window` expands a request accordingly; partial blocks raise `ValueError`. The block deficit is spread by a smooth correction, using Richardson iteration on bilinear coefficients (ω = 1.5, 12 iterations; operator symbol in [1/4, 1]) and then a per-block residual. This is comparable to [Tobler's (1979) pycnophylactic interpolation](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1979.10481647). Fine residual detail is kept, not replaced. Optional hard `lower`/`upper` envelopes (for example `parent_envelope`: parent 3×3 min/max ± margin) are alternated with mean correction. A final step distributes each block's deficit in proportion to its remaining room. This step is exact whenever the parent mean is attainable inside the envelope; the remaining error is reported. Separately processed windows agree only approximately: the correction depends on deficits within about `iterations` blocks. Tiled halos are not claimed to conserve whole-parent means.

**Lakes and still water.** `prepare_water_grid` builds `v = level - ground` (at least +1 mm in owned ocean/lake cells, at most -1 mm elsewhere). Below-zero inland depressions therefore stay dry unless they are lakes. Dry cells within two cells inherit the neighbouring level with fixed neighbour priority. Rasters use bilinear `v` and its gradient for a first-order signed-distance coverage. With target `terrain`, only a fixed band of about one parent cell around the parent shore is re-decided by the fine height relative to the one shared level (1 m soft ramp); owner and level never change. `lake_rings` is marching squares on cell centres with saddle disambiguation by the cell mean and a consistent wet side. Outer rings have positive area and island holes negative area; rings closed by the window edge are flagged `touches_edge`.

**Surface envelopes.** These follow the bed and bank composition in Magna `hydrologySurface.ts`, as a projection rather than an additive cut. River `U`: `level - a D (1 - (2d/W)²)` inside the bankfull width, and `level + (d - W/2) 0.5 / a` outside, where `a = smoothstep(0.5, 2, W / pixel)` is section resolvability. Sub-pixel channels (`a = 0`) are not carved; coverage carries them. Still water with coverage at least 0.5: `U = level - 0.25 m`. Resolved banks within 0.75 W outside the edge: `L = level` when this raises raw ground by at most 2 m. `L <= U` everywhere and `Φ(h) = max(L, min(h, U))`. Envelopes are computed from raw canonical heights and stored. Re-applying them is exactly idempotent; there is no cumulative carve. The delivered class, depth and level are recomputed from the projected height. Still water whose ground remains at or above its level becomes dry.

## Provenance

This adapts ideas and small routines from the local Magna Urbis `city_generator` at HEAD `1d245aa` (same author, GPL-3.0-only): `rust/crates/core/src/hydrology_meanders.rs` (u32 hash, quintic value noise, cubic B-spline course noise, phase/frequency modulation, bounded Hermite tangents), the offset/taper usage in `hydrology.rs`, and bed/bank composition from `rust/bridge/hydrologySurface.ts`. They were rewritten in NumPy. Deviations: angle integration instead of direct offsets for the channel band; wavelengths in channel widths instead of Magna's `30 W` course scale; physical minimum-radius limiting; and a fixed-envelope projection instead of subtracting cuts. Magna's finite-square orchestration, grid-index IDs and repair passes were not copied. The repository's third-party notices already cover GPL Magna components.

## Limitations

- Bends are a graph over the axis: no omega loops, cutoffs, oxbows, bars or secondary channels, and sinuosity rarely exceeds about 1.3. Flow splits and distributaries are not implemented.
- Annual mean Q drives bankfull relations, and width is constant per reach unless the caller passes per-vertex `width_m`.
- Valley fitting uses the supplied terrain inside the corridor only; it does not check divides or basin labels (core/region responsibility). Crossing repair is per-reach damping and does not treat world wrap.
- Rings of a lake crossing the antimeridian are not joined. Fine-shore refinement is local (no connectivity test inside its band).
- Envelope idempotence is guaranteed for stored envelopes from raw heights. The bank-fill threshold depends on raw height, so callers must derive envelopes from raw, never from conditioned output.
- No polar-chart reconciliation, live integration, captures or GPU parity are part of this block.
