# Render v2: implementation and verification

[Français](render-v2-implementation_FR.md)

2026-10-23. Specification: [render-v2.en.md](render-v2.md).

## Results and architecture

A new `render-v2` material and four diagnostics (`render-v2-substrate`, `render-v2-moisture`, `render-v2-vegetation`, `render-v2-snow`) are integrated into Neural Earth and Magna Urbis Terrain Bench. The menu presents one **Render v2** layer; the four diagnostics are a subordinate selector in its settings beside the season. Old diagnostic links remain valid. Historical styles and default engine choices remain available. No agricultural work, weights, DEM, routing or submodule pin is changed.

The NE module `web/terrain_render_v2.js` defines the WGSL evaluator. Its NumPy mirror is `backend/terrain_render_v2.py`. `tools/assets/sync_render_v2.cjs` generates versioned GLSL equations in the neighboring repository; no cross-repository runtime dependency is needed. A shared JSON fixture checks the three evaluators on identical inputs rather than assuming parity.

V2 reuses Render3D's BRDF, analytical normals and crown candidates; historical ground, cover and beaches form the reference. Its microhabitat, close colors, calendar, water composition and diagnostics enrich that reference. It does more than rename Render3D.

The presence of these functions is not visual acceptance. The [real-tile audit](render-v2-audit-40km.md) retains baseline regressions and comparisons. Render and Render3D remain the quality threshold: beaches, sediment banks and terrain shadows must remain readable. Better palette or detail does not compensate for their disappearance: comparison uses identical coordinates, tiles and extents, and a successful feature of historical rendering becomes a non-regression criterion.

### Current state: corrections and validation on real tiles

- Six V2 control layers, including seasonal temperatures, are transported in `rgba16float`; Render3D retains its historical `rgba8unorm` transport. This reduces thermal quantization without changing physical tiles.
- Rainfall transport uses the tile's actual seasonal thermal gradients. A constant lapse rate must not replace those data.
- The V2 calendar also drives the inherited ground material. Changing date recomputes its required color textures while reusing physical textures and terrain; no new decoding is requested.
- Physical beach excludes vegetation cover. Sediment flats use a separate mask and can retain estuarine vegetation. Resolved tree exclusion is decided at the root using a fixed 30 m reference mask. Multiplying reveal by the pixel's beach fraction made crowns translucent in sand; that defect was removed. Six real coastal captures at 2.56 km and 160 m are retained in `output/render-v2-floor/beach-root-final/`, with two pixel-identical Render3D controls. Survivors retain opacity; the distant average retains its area-coverage approximation.
- Ground detail passes through open-ground and litter blends. Mineral normals do not become crown normals.
- The closed guide retains canopy color before historical seasons and snow. The filtered macro gradient has its own transport: its lighting normalization must not remove fine DEM slopes.
- Mineral modulation is continuous and low amplitude. The old clamp saturated the shape before display and produced two flat colors. Snow retains relief through compression of high values after lighting rather than clipping each channel.
- Average cover uses the population that actually generates crowns. Open ground, litter and canopy are lit separately, then mixed in linear light; resolved details converge to this average according to footprint. The coarse bake retains this unclipped response in half-float. It nevertheless uses a regional stand expectation, not exact integration of fine candidates.
- Forest disturbances at 100–400 m now thin cover rather than emptying entire cells: `min(.55, 2*c*sigmoid(score/1.15))`. Rate `c` remains tied to the control and environment; it is reduced in humid forest. Physical openings and gaps between candidates remain distinct. Ablation on real CFB tiles removes small ochre spots without global darkening, with pixel-identical Render3D controls.
- Stand density variation acts on optical cover (`1 - (1-cover)^density`) rather than directly multiplying and saturating cover percentage. The latter carved large light networks into cold forests at LOD3. The formula is a saturating appearance control, not a LAI measurement.
- Snow is composited above litter in fine and coarse paths. The full-snow GPU check compares 2,098 forest-floor pixels with open snowy ground: zero albedo difference. Litter must no longer reintroduce brown above that snow.
- Snow cover retains an annual component: the maximum of seasonal snow and a floor driven by the warmest month, moisture and slope retention. This floor uses the historical material's permanent thermal interval without an added biome mask or stand noise. It corrects a real EF plain that turned grey-beige in summer in rejected pass `89516…`. Evidence `2b6b939d8e0e…` contains 27 real images at three scales: all three EF July V2 views recover light cover; the 18 DFC/arid controls remain pixel-identical. Render3D and EF January are also unchanged. Receipt and comparison are in `output/render-v2-snow-annual-reference/`. This remains a climate approximation without an interannual accumulation or melt budget.

These corrections still require a full visual comparison after shader stabilization, especially for tropical-forest exposure.

Current tests cover 77 Python rendering cases (27 historical, 8 Torch, 42 V2), GPU compilation of fine and coarse paths, temperature/rain transport, season change without another physical upload, beaches and ground/crown isolation. They do not replace acceptance of real images. Older capture and performance figures below describe their respective versions.

Canopy transport also fixes channel confusion: riverbank moisture occupied the historical forest-tone channel. It must no longer darken an opaque crown. The GPU isolation check gives zero difference between the two riverbank moisture values for those pixels. The ecological diagnostic field now uses the same effective cover as the color.

The prepared campaign contains 60 land/coastal positions and six hydrological situations, each at five extents and in three views (V2 July, Render3D July, V2 January). Final images have not yet been accepted: working directories must not be presented as the 990 final captures. The manifest distinguishes requested relief from measured relief; four plateau/biome combinations are not strictly covered.

The server reload needed for lake rasterization changed physical identity from `f5cddc…` to `bd3673…`: the provenance manifest also includes `index.html` and the server. Height, climate and erosion sources remain identical. Old caches and evidence are not renamed; the new campaign recomputes its real tiles and retains its own receipts.

## Environmental evaluator

Inputs: canonical position in meters, seed, physical elevation/gradient, local topographic position, temperature and rainfall for the two reference seasons, regional sand, riparian envelope and annual date. Outputs: local temperature, potential/current moisture, exposure, mineral province, sand/clay/organic matter, outcrop, grass cover, woody potential, height, conifer share, leaf-out, snow, freezing potential and stand tone. The separate glacial-provenance field is described below.

The 180/690 km provinces mix black/grey, brown, beige/limestone and iron red without the historical material's grey neutralization. A seed-dependent domain translation prevents all small local maps around the origin from sharing the same province. The 2.4 km and 670 m mosaics modestly modulate microhabitat with bottoms, ridges and slopes. They are appearance proxies, not new weather or measured geological maps.

Sand, clay and organic matter are bounded and sum to no more than one. Rock and grasses share the ground; canopy is a separate layer. Snow is continuous retention depending on cold, supply and slope, without a noise threshold scattering isolated white patches. Sand remains a family of materials: no geometric dunes or fictitious incisions are added.

Bands are filtered according to pixel footprint without renormalizing remaining octaves. Microhabitat noise disappears in distant views; it is not sampled unfiltered at 20 km/pixel. Fine normals retain Render3D integer/fraction coordinates. Grass cover has low normal gain and sward variation bounded to 2.5%, without turning small crowns into grass blades.

## Calendar, stands and diagnostics

The visible **Season** slider has its own `v2season` state, restored from the URL and recipes. The historical slider is hidden during v2 display. The calendar drives leaf color and opacity, shadows, grass cover, moisture, snow and ice. Candidates, control textures and seeds are not rebuilt when the slider moves. December/January joins exactly. In Magna, moving the season updates map/perspective uniforms without rebuilding projected textures. NE uses spatial seasonal references, so hemispheres follow their own calendars. The hydrological request month and water cache key change at monthly boundaries; this does not rerun inference. With hydrology active, those boundaries can request new water buffers over the local network and re-upload a combined tile; the calendar probe's verified absence of a physical upload concerns the material without that monthly change. There is no continuous per-frame hydrological simulation.

The four diagnostics call the same evaluator as final color. They show substrate, local moisture, vegetation layers and snow/ice. Water ice cover calls the same `v2Ice` / `ice_material` compositor as the final material, including floes and icebergs. It is not merely a palette of freezing potential. These do not replace historical physical climate diagnostics, which retain their regional meaning.

The stable v2 field reuses Render3D's deterministic crown candidates and ecotones. NE's riparian gallery is a narrow envelope (65 m maximum in this raster adaptation) tied to authoritative river samples. Magna retains its existing geometric envelope, applied occupancy and building exclusions. A root in water is rejected; water is composited last. Seasonal leaf withdrawal also reduces cover and shadows, without drawing new tree positions.

## Water, ice and iceberg provenance

Physical RGBA carries level, wet fraction, class and authority. An authoritative dry sample remains land even below sea level. V2 composites water according to substrate, moisture, depth and channel fraction. Shallow edge and deep center differ. At sea, after shallow-water absorption, a bathymetric tint retains continental-shelf through abyssal nuances from 150 to 3,000 m. This preserves Render3D's depth cue in wide views; it does not make the abyssal seabed optically visible. Lakes and channels do not receive this marine tint. Lake, river and sea freezing have distinct thresholds; moving water freezes more cautiously. Cold drives slightly greater concentration in shallow coastal water. Depth is an available coastal proxy, not a new geometric distance to the coast.

Compact sea ice retains large adjoining floes of about 1.2 km, mildly warped (65 m), separated by narrow channels and their ridges. Closed noise isolines initially used as small cracks were removed: they produced artificial contours visible at 5 m/pixel. The less concentrated margin selects stable opaque floes: 2.6 km parents, 470 m subfragments in some parents, independent XYZ warps of 215 m. The marginal correction removes whole semitransparent floes and cyan networks under absent fragments: binary selection, rim limited to ice present. The compact/marginal transition is continuous between concentrations 0.68 and 0.90.

The cellular field compares the two nearest distances among 27 points in a deterministically displaced 3D grid. Margin, icebergs and inland ice keep their `F2-F1` contours. Compact marine pack uses distance to the two sites' bisector: `(d2²-d1²)/(2|site2-site1|)`. This prevents fracture width from expanding into pockets near vertices. Channels a few meters wide are integrated over the pixel footprint, and the secondary refrozen-ice network is attenuated. Compression relief stays narrow; none of these floes displaces the DEM. The generator adapts the cellular basis of [Worley (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1145/237170.237267), without its general Poisson seeding. Coordinates are spherical in NE and regional metric in Magna; no local tile seed is introduced. Edge widths grow with pixel footprint; between 140 and 450 m/pixel, cover, brightness and rims converge toward a calibrated analytical average. This is not an exact area integral of each floe. Beyond that, cells and strong provincial variations are no longer sampled. None of these effects displaces the DEM or water levels.

NE reuses monthly flows when available. A uniform fallback series does not prove a dry wadi. Fast hydrology supplies v2 an authority raster without carving: physical elevation remains original. Historical blue overlays do not repaint areas already composited by v2.

On the fast path, `v2_lakes` adds a raster of large source lakes (default threshold 1 km²) to the V2 compositor. Only DEM points below lake level are wet; islands remain dry and cut/fill is strictly zero. This option does not activate historical small local depressions. Close up, within existing lake support, the shore is the water plane's intersection with the displayed DEM, antialiased according to gradient and pixel. It no longer dilates the bilinear 30 m fraction across the whole shore. The transition progressively retains that filtered fraction between 15 and 60 m/px; sea and channels are unaffected by this lake-only treatment. Native coarse paths at LOD 4 and above still use vectors: their lake freezing is not covered by the new raster. A genuinely recomputed 30 m tile under `bd3673…` verifies 26,395 lake samples, valid authority and no change to the DEM or legacy output.

Transport channel 50, added to the 50 historical channels, is canonical proximity to potential glacial coasts. It is calculated and cached from the source atlas using coastal land, perpetually cold summer and minimum supply. It is continuous at the antimeridian and in the polar chart; it does not depend on displayed tile edges. See the [provenance method](../methods/render-v2-glacier-provenance.md). Icebergs use eroded, lobed cellular fragments of varied sizes up to several hundred meters rather than disks. Provenance controls selection density, not surface transparency. A white plateau, darker sides, submerged cyan rim and oriented shadow give satellite-view volume. Shadow is derived from the same cell without a second cellular search. Depth attenuates provenance while still allowing deep fjords. Marine cold alone creates no iceberg. Between 115 and 290 m/pixel, blocks converge toward their approximate mean area. These are localized proxies without simulated calving, drift, currents or thermodynamics. Visual references are the [NSIDC stages](https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/sea-ice/science-sea-ice) and [NASA IceBridge aerial atlas](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11143).

Magna uses at most eight groups of cold coastal source cells from the retained field, prepared once on the CPU; its fragments do not search hundreds of terrain samples for a coast. Its climate is an annual preset: the northern summer/winter adaptation uses continental amplitude `6 + 0.5 max(0, 10 - T_annual)`. It does not reconstruct a planetary climate.

## Distant view, close view and outputs

NE uses the same large-scale environment for CPU/PNG overviews, coarse blocks, WebGPU quads and globe. The distance does not draw unresolved individual trees or stones. There are no three artificially independent Orogen/Coarse/Base layers: their data and footprint determine visible frequencies. Geometric terrain detail remains that of the existing stages.

In Magna, map and perspective use the same GLSL, forest fields and retained masks. Raster/export uses the same material and its integration at the requested resolution; it does not claim pixel-for-pixel crown equality between an export and a camera at a different resolution. V2 seas remain in the full raster: neither extension of land colors nor a land clip erases them. The English note `city_generator/docs/methods/render-v2.md` is embedded in the method viewer and accessible via the season/style help button, including offline.

## Corrections from user review

The first correction pass retained Render3D open, grass, outcrop, woody cover and beach colors at medium and long distance. Palette substitution between 6 and 1.5 m/pixel has since been removed: the inherited open-ground average is retained at all footprints, enriched by bounded mineral variation before sediment, beach and snow. Fine decoder slope no longer triggers general substitution of green by v2 rock. Canopy remains seasonal. Riparian enhancement respects cold, perennial snow and thermal height without creating crowns in EF environments.

Mineral provinces remain fixed; their displayed contrast fades between 600 and 2400 m/pixel. Iron reds are desaturated. Regional modeling returns to canonical hillshade, with the user's exaggeration, ambient and contrast settings; the BRDF adds only local responses. Cold snow can fully cover flat terrain; it attenuates mineral grain instead of always leaving 18% substrate visible.

Local land noise, including inherited ground noise, uses a cylindrical domain in the DEM chart. The polar control at 85° and 41.9 m/pixel changes from a horizontal/vertical variation ratio of 0.0765 to 0.973 for partial snow; bare ground is close to 0.99. Marine fields are evaluated separately in spherical coordinates. Fine sea-ice settings retain adjoining floes and move their edges together with a vector field rather than varying only the channel threshold. Matte relief remains weak; there is no fracture dynamics or photorealistic validation of these floes.

Post-correction captures `green-after-v2.png`, `coast-after-v2.png` and their Render3D comparisons show the two real decoder sites at identical camera and lighting. `green-lod3-after-*` captures also retain the same camera with forced 240 m latent. The ice site is checked at real LOD −1 (15 m, 84 patches), at 24.43 m/pixel. Iteration names and reports are under `output/render-v2-review/`; intermediate captures are not a promise of visual compliance for every map.

In Magna, the map/perspective/raster paths evaluate the historical material once and pass its pre-snow ground to v2. Beach is retained and v2 calendar snow is then composited. The shared call avoids duplicating large forest functions in the shader.

## Measured costs and limitations

On revision `15f91fbd07b9…`, after stand, snow/litter and beach corrections, the RTX 3090 benchmark with no other GPU work measures on its 1920 × 1080 fixture: bake **0.900 ms/GPU tile**, upload with wait **2.050 ms/tile**, draw at 16 / 4 / 1 / 0.25 / 0.02 m/px: **1.289 / 4.627 / 1.782 / 5.837 / 6.910 ms GPU**. Season then draw: **2.019 ms GPU**. Initialization: 5.181 s; shared lazy compilation: 8.119 s. Report: `output/render-v2-floor/final-benchmark-15f91fbd.json`. This fixture does not activate a hydrological network: it does not measure regional-mask regeneration when the camera moves. A nonempty mask occupies about 12 bytes per pixel plus segments, or 12.58 MB at 1024²; the valid empty mask measures 1 × 1. These figures are not a navigation frame rate including generation, network and browser composition.

On frozen sources `db083bf3841a…`, the same benchmark was rerun without another GPU capture or active neural computation. On RTX 3090, 1920×1080 fixture: V2 bake **0.902 ms/GPU tile**, upload with completion wait **1.944 ms/tile**; draw at 16 / 4 / 1 / 0.25 / 0.02 m/px: **1.114 / 4.006 / 1.610 / 5.121 / 6.692 ms GPU**. Season change and draw: **1.839 ms GPU**. Initialization: 6.776 s; shared lazy compilation: 7.797 s. Report: `output/render-v2-floor/final-benchmark.json`. Costs are not monotonic with zoom: statistical average and resolved crowns take different branches. GPU timestamps and wall-clock medians are separate measurements; this probe measures neither inference nor network nor complete navigation frame rate. The current polar probe measures 0.691 ms GPU for 2,359,296 pixels; dispatch and CPU readback take 40.3 ms.

Before the polar revision, on RTX 3090/Ampere and a synthetic 1920×1080 fixture, the existing benchmark run with `node tools/benchmarks/benchmark_render_layer.cjs --render-v2` measured separate GPU timestamps. Bake: 0.480 ms/tile; upload and fence: 0.922 ms/tile. Draw at 16 / 4 / 1 / 0.25 / 0.02 m/pixel: 0.882 / 2.631 / 3.274 / 5.943 / 7.324 ms. Season change followed by draw measured 2.894 ms on that fixture without rebaking the stable field. Shared lazy compilation: about 4.9 s cold, initialization about 3.2 s. These are neither a 60 fps guarantee nor a full generation, network transfer, browser composition and presentation time. The final polar probe measured a 0.637 ms GPU timestamp for 2,359,296 synthetic-atlas pixels on the same card; 43.8 ms for dispatch and reading 32 MB includes CPU transfer. This isolated measurement is not application frame rate. The GPU bypasses water on land pixels; the CPU skips water composition on entirely dry surfaces. Cellular search is also skipped when pack or provenance are absent and beyond their integration footprints. Small SwiftShader GLSL compilation probe timings are not user GPU measurements.

Explicit limitations:

- The minimum lake-depth filter affects the historical overlay but is not passed to the V2 lake raster. Its value does not yet filter those surfaces; the area filter has its dedicated parameter. River-junction review confirmed this distinct defect without “fixing” it by approximately excluding all lakes. Reference captures use the default zero threshold.
- The model remains synthetic natural appearance, without new historical weather, snow accumulation, sediment transport or measured geology.
- Source climate, flow and paths retain their available precision. Refinement does not invent missing seasonal discharge. Magna has no monthly discharge series: no river there is arbitrarily dried by local aridity alone.
- Distant cover is a calibrated average appearance, not exact conservation of each cellular field's area. Concentration follows available climate and coastal depth; no ocean heat or interannual ice memory is available.
- Iceberg normals and caps are satellite-material effects, not 3D geometric silhouettes or drift simulation.
- Terrestrial microhabitat and inherited local noises follow the metric of the chart carrying the DEM, with a longitude-periodic cylindrical domain. Broad provinces and marine materials retain the canonical sphere. On the globe, relief and texture undergo the same projection; this does not make the cartographic DEM intrinsically metric across the whole sphere. Transition between normal and polar charts can change local terrestrial detail: its identity across charts is not guaranteed.
- Stable-field gradients/TPI remain those of physical tiles and their halos; a new DEM/LOD can still slightly change tree eligibility at an edge. Seeds and candidate positions are stable, but absolute crown invariance across all refinements is not demonstrated. No perfect parity between DEMs of different resolutions is claimed.
- In Magna perspective, an inland-water texture without a separate class is conservatively treated as moving water for freezing. Its inland depth inferred from cover is an appearance proxy, not bathymetry. The CPU preview represents average cover, not each close crown's geometry.
- This machine's hardware ANGLE/D3D compiler also hangs in a TerrainGl constructor probe from HEAD (no terrain generation), with a 60 s watchdog; the current constructor reaches the same watchdog. The HEAD probe retains only small uniform/attenuation additions from the shared forest module: it is not a measurement of a fully isolated baseline. ANGLE Vulkan on NVIDIA RTX 3090 did compile the real application and render summer/winter map and perspective without error. The limit concerns this machine's D3D backend; no universal frame rate or hardware perspective performance is inferred from SwiftShader checks.

Appearance references are [PBRT Noise](https://pbr-book.org/3ed-2018/Texture/Noise), [Filament](https://google.github.io/filament/main/filament.html) and [NSIDC Sea Ice](https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/sea-ice). Adaptations use spatial consistency, filtering and material/lighting separation; they do not implement those references' complete physical models.
